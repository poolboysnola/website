Why weekly

A pool doesn't drift out of balance politely.

Water chemistry moves every single day. Sun burns off chlorine, rain dilutes everything, swimmers add their own contribution, and a New Orleans summer manages all three before lunch. Leave it two weeks and the water isn't "a little off" — it's cloudy, itchy, and well on its way to green.

Weekly service is just staying in front of that. We test, adjust, clean, and look over the equipment while the problem is still a number on a test panel instead of a weekend of shocking and scrubbing.

The boring side effect is that your equipment lasts longer. Clear baskets and a filter that isn't choking keep the pump from working overtime, and a pump that isn't working overtime doesn't quit on you in July.

Every visit

Seven things, every single time.

No "as needed" asterisks on the basics. This is the whole list, and it's the same list whether you're a courtyard plunge pool or a 40-footer in Lakeview.

Skim the surface

Leaves, live-oak debris, bugs, and whatever the last storm dropped in. Anything that sinks before we get there stops being litter and starts being algae food.

Vacuum the floor

Dirt settles in the deep end and the corners your robot politely ignores. We pull it out so it isn't feeding bacteria until the next visit.

Brush walls, steps, and ledges

Algae starts where the water moves slowest — shaded walls, tanning ledges, behind the ladder. Brushing breaks the film up before it gets a grip, and keeps calcium from etching a waterline.

Empty the baskets

Two minutes that save your pump. A packed skimmer or pump basket starves the pump of water, and a starved pump runs hot and dies early.

Run the full test panel

pH, free chlorine, total alkalinity, calcium hardness, cyanuric acid — plus salt if you run a cell. We test what actually explains the water, not just the chlorine.

Balance it, in the right order

Alkalinity before pH, stabilizer before the sun eats your chlorine. Order matters more than dose, which is why chasing one number at a time never quite works. Chemicals are in your rate.

Look over the equipment

Pump, filter pressure, heater, salt cell, automation. We're listening for a bearing whine and looking for the small leak, because those are cheap this week and expensive next month.

Before you book

The questions we get most.

Do I need to be home?

No. A gate code or a lockbox is plenty. You get a written service note after every visit saying what we tested, what we added, and anything we want to keep an eye on — so you're never guessing what happened in your own backyard.

Are chemicals included?

Yes, in your regular rate. No surprise line item for the chlorine we were always going to add. If your pool needs something genuinely out of the ordinary — a heavy phosphate treatment, say — we'll tell you the price before we open the bottle.

What happens after a big rain or a storm?

Rain dilutes your chemistry and drops half a tree in the water, so the visit after a storm is usually a longer one. Ahead of a named storm we'll secure loose equipment, set the water level, and shock the pool. Cleanups afterward are prioritized by damage, not by route order.

My pool is already green. Should I start here?

Start with a recovery, then go weekly. Trying to claw back a green pool on a weekly cadence is slower and costs more than clearing it properly once and holding it there.

Do you cover my street?

Probably — we run routes from Metairie to the Bywater and up over the lake. Check the service area, and if you're just outside the lines, ask anyway. We make exceptions.
